Forgot Your Safe Combination? What to Do Next

By Uncle Thomas, locksmith since 1998 · Updated October 2026

📞 Call 9224 9224💬 WhatsApp Us

Forgetting a safe code happens more often than you'd think — after a long holiday, after inheriting a safe, or when the person who set it has left the company. Here's what Uncle Thomas, a US-trained safesmith, suggests.

1. Check the batteries (electronic safes)

A weak battery can make an electronic keypad behave strangely — accepting the code but not releasing the bolt. Many safes have an external battery point or a battery compartment you can reach from outside. Fresh batteries fix more "forgotten code" problems than you'd expect.

2. Look for the override key

Most home and hotel-style electronic safes come with an emergency override key, usually behind a small cover near the keypad. Check drawers, the original box and paperwork.

3. Contact the manufacturer

Some brands can provide a reset code or replacement key if you register the safe and prove ownership — have the serial number and your purchase receipt ready.

4. Don't try to drill or force it

Safes are designed to resist attack. Drilling in the wrong place, prying the door or hammering the bolts can trigger relockers that make the safe much harder — and more expensive — to open, and can damage what's inside.

5. Call a safesmith

A trained safesmith can usually open a safe with minimal damage. Uncle Thomas trained in safesmithing in the United States and has opened safes for multinational companies, on ships and across Asia. See safe opening in Singapore.
