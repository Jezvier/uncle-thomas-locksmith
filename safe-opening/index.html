Why a safesmith, not just a locksmith

Opening a safe is a different skill from opening a door. Safes are built to resist forced entry, so the wrong approach can destroy the safe — or its contents. In Singapore, few locksmiths have formal safe training. Uncle Thomas spent a year in the United States training in safesmithing, and has since been called to open safes for multinational companies, on board ships and cruise liners, and in Thailand, Malaysia, China and Hong Kong.

Safes we open

  • Home safes — electronic keypad, key and combination safes.
  • Hotel-style and wardrobe safes.
  • Office and business safes — cash safes, drop safes and document safes.
  • Older mechanical dial safes.
  • Safes on ships and vessels.

Common reasons people call us

  • Forgot the combination or PIN.
  • Lost the key, or the override key.
  • Electronic keypad dead even after changing the batteries.
  • Bolt jammed — the code is accepted but the door won't open.
  • Inherited a safe and no one knows the code.

Before you call, try the steps in our guide: forgot your safe combination — what to do.

Proof of ownership: Before opening any safe we confirm it belongs to you or your business — for example a purchase receipt, company authorisation or proof of residence where the safe is kept. Jobs are handled discreetly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you open a safe without destroying it?

In many cases, yes — that's what safesmith training is for. The method depends on the safe. We'll explain the approach and price before we start.

I forgot my safe code. Can you help?

Yes. Try the manufacturer's reset steps first (see our guide). If that doesn't work, WhatsApp us a photo of the safe and we'll quote you.

Do you open safes for businesses?

Yes — offices, shops and vessels. We'll need authorisation from the business before opening.

How much does safe opening cost?

It's quoted per job, depending on the safe. Send a photo on WhatsApp to 9224 9224 for a price.
